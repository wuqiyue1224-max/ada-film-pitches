01 / A Note to Your Film
You can’t cut
someone into love.

Dear Director and Producer,

Norman edits Lena’s life like a timeline: bad coffee, gone. A creepy stranger, gone. A cruel boss, gone. Each murder is an impeccably efficient deletion. But her freedom to choose is the one thing he cannot edit away.

I’m excited by the contrast between his surgical precision and the uncontrollable messiness of affection. The comedy can be beautifully exact, while the final “Mission resumed” leaves a little chill: nothing about him has actually changed.

03 / A Little About Me

Editor by craft.
Story lover
by nature.

I’m Ada Qiyue Wu, an MFA filmmaker at the USC School of Cinematic Arts. I come to the editing room with a director’s curiosity, an editor’s patience, and a genuine affection for emotional detail.

Editing has been the thread running through my film education and practice. I began in the inaugural Editing Director cohort at the Communication University of Zhejiang and kept developing my craft through shorts, documentaries, and feature-film post-production. Before choosing USC, I received formal offers from the film editing programs at AFI Conservatory and Chapman University. In my MFA training, I've studied under Duwayne Dunham, and my mentor is Bartholomew Burcham.

Across narrative shorts, feature post-production, sound, and visual effects, I’m most interested in helping a film become more itself — clearer in feeling, sharper in rhythm, and more specific in voice.
